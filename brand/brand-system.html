Idea

A carnival is a system of attractions. The brand prints it like a festival bill.

Midway. Web3 Carnival is a festival series, not a single summit: a flagship week with daily side events, then stops in Dubai, Singapore and Delhi. The identity borrows from printed festival culture: ticket stock, lineup posters, destination boards and stamps. It is set with Swiss-grid discipline so it reads premium, not kitsch.

Intersection layer. Discovery uses the role filter. A visitor picks who they are (Founder × Investor, for example). Sessions, speakers and side events then rank by how many chosen roles they serve. The × glyph is the only Web3-native device in the system.

Raised from the challenger round. Bombay painted poster: billing is size, and yellow is reserved for the primary action. Depot destination blind: city and event names step one course at a time with an overshoot, and nothing glides. Nixie counter: figures sit at fixed tabular positions like ticket serials, never as a hero-metric strip. Tensegrity column: filter results have three distinct states (both roles, one role, neither), not a vague highlight.

Rules

What keeps it premium.

Logo. Use the official tile as supplied. Never recolour, outline or stretch it. The poster lockup ("WEB3 / CARNIVAL" in Big Shoulders) is a proposed secondary treatment for headlines only, never a replacement mark. The Threeway Studio credit moves to the footer.

Photography. Speaker headshots come from many sources, so they are unified as grayscale 4:5 crops on a stock colour. Event photography stays in full colour, is cropped 3:2 or 21:9, and is not filtered. Real photos are missing today, and every slot shows a labelled placeholder.

Refusals. No purple, gradients, glass, 3D coins, neon glow or terminal styling. The × glyph is the only crypto-native mark. Carnival devices (stubs, stamps, blinds) carry function every time; there is no decorative bunting.

Truth. Next-edition facts render as "TBC" until confirmed. Figures carry the note "as reported by Web3 Carnival".
